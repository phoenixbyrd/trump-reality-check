'Sloppy and lazy': Kash Patel humiliated after teen hackers breach the FBI and steal personal info of thousands of agents
UnPresidented · 2026-09-27● MisleadingRead the original →

The claim: Teen hackers (ShinyHunters) breached FBI internal systems and stole personal data of thousands of FBI agents -- stated as fact in the piece, while Reuters/AP reporting only partially verified sample records and had not established that the data came from FBI internal systems.

Why misleading: The UnPresidented piece states as established fact — in its headline and body — that ShinyHunters 'breached the FBI' and 'stole a massive collection of sensitive data from the agency, including the personal information of thousands of agents.' The named-source record does not support that framing. Reuters (Raphael Satter and AJ Vicens, Sept. 22) reported the group's claim and partially verified a sample of ~5,000 records — finding matches in at least 10 instances, including FBI Director Kash Patel, by running details against credit bureau records and the dark-web intelligence firm District 4 Labs — but explicitly wrote: 'the news agency could not establish where the data came from, or whether it had been stolen from the FBI's internal systems as the hackers claimed.' 404 Media, which first reported the claim, similarly verified only that some sample records matched real people via OSINT tools. The FBI's September 23 statement said it is 'aware of claims regarding unauthorized activity affecting FBIjobs.gov and is currently investigating' and, per cybersecurity outlet SecurityAffairs, the agency has not determined whether the point of breach was a third party or its own enterprise and has not confirmed its internal systems were compromised. The verdict is misleading rather than false: the piece's core assertion (the FBI was breached and thousands of agents' data stolen) is presented as fact, while every named source — including the Reuters reporting the piece itself cites — stops at 'claimed' and explicitly says the source of the data is unconfirmed.

Sources: Reuters (via LA Post syndication) SecurityAffairs (Pierluigi Paganini, FBI statement) SecureWorld (FBI Sept. 23 statement, verification summary)
