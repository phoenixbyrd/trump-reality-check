Fact Check: Is Trump banning federal loans for students in low-earning college majors?
MEAWW · 2026-09-29● MisleadingRead the original →

The claim: Trump is banning students majoring in degrees that don't make enough money from taking out college loans; degrees for social work, art, religious studies, teaching aides, and music will be hit the hardest. (The New Republic's September 28 X post, 20M+ views, amplified by FactPost and others.)

Why misleading: The viral 'ban' framing materially misstates how the policy works, but it is built on a real rule — so 'false' would overstate the case the other way. The Education Department finalized an earnings-accountability rule (the 'Do No Harm' test, implementing provisions of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act): undergraduate programs whose graduates' median earnings fall below those of typical high-school-diploma holders aged 25–34 — measured via IRS data four years after graduation — in two of three consecutive years lose access to federal Direct Loans; graduate programs face a higher benchmark against bachelor's-degree holders. The earliest penalties land in the 2028–29 academic year, and no program or major is 'banned' — students may still enroll, and the federal government does not set curricula, as the Education Department stressed in its September 28 X post calling the claim 'fake news.' Yet the functional consequence the viral posts point to is real: Inside Higher Ed and The Source confirm programs that fail the test do lose federal Direct Loan eligibility, which can put affected degrees out of reach for students who depend on federal borrowing. 'Trump is banning students' turns a program-level accountability mechanism into a student-level ban, which is why the honest rating is misleading rather than outright false.

How we rate

  • What this site is. Every day we collect the most-trending claims about President Trump — actions attributed to him, quotes attributed to him — largely from outlets critical of him, and check each claim against named, published sources.
  • What we check. The central factual claim — and we check whether it is actually true, not merely whether someone said it. "X says Y happened" proves only that X said it. A claim earns "Accurate" only when the underlying event is confirmed by direct evidence: primary documents, official records, direct video or audio, on-the-ground wire reporting. When the core assertion can neither be confirmed nor denied, it gets "Unverifiable" — never a guess. "Why this rating" walks through the evidence step by step, with the key numbers, dates, and quotes. Pure opinion pieces with no checkable factual claim get "Opinion — no checkable claim" instead of a fake verdict.
  • Honest verdicts, both directions. A biased outlet can still report facts accurately — when the claim holds up, it gets "Accurate", full stop. The mission is what's factual and what isn't, not debunking everything. We don't infer motives, and when sources disagree or something is unknown, we say so.
  • Read the original. Every story links the original article so you can check our work.
